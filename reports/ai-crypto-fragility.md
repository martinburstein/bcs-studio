# Conditional Capability, Exposure, Adaptation, and Loss in Cryptographic Transitions

## A scenario framework for AI-enabled cryptanalysis and cryptocurrency fragility

**Public working paper, version 0.1-public (14 November 2026). Unsubmitted. No external peer review.** This is an AI-assisted research synthesis and proposed conditional model, not an established unified discipline, an investment recommendation, or a forecast. No attack implementation, private key, account record, trade, or personal balance is included.

## Abstract

AI-assisted cryptanalysis and post-quantum resource estimates motivate a conditional economic question: when could a demonstrated technical capability affect claims in a deployed system before defenders adapt? A complete answer requires separate measurements of (i) technical eligibility for an exact target and goal, (ii) a dated exposed claimant cohort, (iii) an institutional mechanism that can transmit an accepted event, (iv) the defender's adaptation and containment state, and (v) a claimant-level economic outcome measured independently of those gates. These variables are often endogenous and correlated. This paper therefore estimates no attack probability, arrival date, exposure share, adaptation effect, market impact, or aggregate loss.

The paper offers a proposed reporting protocol, not a new general theory or a calibrated empirical result. The protocol links versioned capability evidence to a deployment cohort, transition incentives, a pre-event exposure denominator, specified settlement/claim mechanisms, and net realized outcomes. Two small migration games illustrate the difference between complementary steps and a shared-provider architecture; they are finite model lemmas, not novel general theorems or descriptions of a named blockchain. A synthetic loss example reports arithmetic only. A separate, associational hypothesis specifies clocks, denominator, outcome window, comparison and exclusions for a possible future cohort study; it is explicitly not identified here. The strongest countercases are perfect secrecy for a narrow one-use-pad model, fast or pre-emptive migration, capable defenders, localized exposure and recoveries. The paper supports neither inevitable AI-enabled cryptographic failure nor a cryptocurrency-collapse forecast or trade.

## 1. Introduction

Technical capability and economic consequence are different observations. A result may be mathematically correct but concern another primitive, parameter set or attack goal. An applicable method may require unavailable inputs or resources. A feasible attack may face no vulnerable deployment, may be rejected by a verifier, or may encounter an effective pause, migration, recovery or compensation mechanism. Even when an unauthorized event is accepted, its financial meaning depends on who has a claim, whether that claim is reversible, how it is funded, and what value is recovered. A price movement is not itself proof of technical compromise; a technical compromise need not produce material net claimant loss.

AI language can blur these distinctions. Research assistance, benchmark success, reduced-instance results, asymptotic algorithms, modeled resource cost, full-parameter demonstrations, and exploitation of a deployed system are not interchangeable evidence classes. A system that improves an attacker's search may also improve defensive audit, testing, migration and recovery. The sign and magnitude of any net effect depend on task, access, resources, institutions and relative response time. This paper reports no coefficient for offensive or defensive AI productivity and makes no claim that AI has broken deployed cryptocurrency cryptography.

The paper develops a conditional measurement framework for a narrow path: a specified capability may yield an accepted event against an exposed deployment; an institutional arrangement can then transmit some part of it to a claimant; adaptation and recovery change what remains. Each arrow needs a source and a measurement. The framework can be used for descriptive incident accounting, a preregistered cohort study, or a decision scenario. It cannot turn missing data into a probability. All scenario parameters in the example are synthetic.

This work revises an earlier private laboratory manuscript and its mathematical artifacts after an internal methodological challenge. Prior work called the research program “Cryptographic Transition Economics” (CTE). That label is a proposed organizing shorthand, not an established academic field or a priority claim. Earlier AI-assisted internal reviews share models and context; they are not independent external replications. This public editorial edition remains unsubmitted. It contains no account information, private portfolio values, live quotes, trading proposal, cryptographic attack code or proprietary claims.

## 2. Relation to prior literature and contribution

Security-economics work has long argued that security outcomes can reflect incentives, liability, information asymmetry and externalities as well as engineering. Anderson's 2001 ACSAC paper, *Why Information Security is Hard—An Economic Perspective* [1], supplies a direct antecedent on security incentives, externalities and liability. It does not measure cryptocurrency losses. Varian's *System Reliability and Free Riding* [2] distinguishes total-effort, weakest-link and best-shot reliability technologies and discusses voluntary provision and liability.

Migration and standardization research likewise precedes the present model. Farrell and Saloner (1985) [3] analyze compatibility benefits, switching and excess inertia, including incomplete information; their result warns that adoption is architecture- and information-dependent. It is not a measured cryptographic transition lag. Diamond and Dybvig's (1983) demand-deposit model [4] and Allen and Gale's (2000) overlapping-claims model [5] supply conditional mechanisms for runs and contagion; they require particular contracts, liquidity preferences or balance-sheet links. Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2009) [6] connect funding and market liquidity through margins and intermediary capacity under stated model conditions. These mechanisms cannot be applied to a crypto asset merely because its price falls or users seek exit.

Shannon's 1949 primary result [7] is used only as a counterexample to universal claims that computation alone defeats every secrecy system: under its defined model, perfect secrecy means the ciphertext distribution is independent of the message. It says nothing about authentication, key handling, implementation leakage or deployed cryptocurrency design. The result is limited to its formal confidentiality model; it does not establish properties of authentication, key handling, implementations or deployed cryptocurrencies.

The proposed contribution is modest: a reusable measurement and reporting bridge across these literatures. It requires a dated capability claim, exact target and parameters, access/resource scope, exposure denominator, adaptation state, an independently coded claimant outcome, and the institutions that connect them. The sequence is a checklist for evidence and measurement, not a novel theorem. The B and E games below instantiate two architectures already familiar from complementary-effort and public-goods/reliability analysis. No claim of comprehensive literature coverage or priority is made.

## 3. Definitions and causal model

### 3.1 Unit, clocks and independently defined variables

The unit is an adversarial path `j` applied to construction and parameters `k`, implementation/version `v`, claimant cohort `c`, and observation window `t`. The attack goal must be specified: confidentiality, integrity/authentication, availability, consensus, data availability, or another outcome. These goals have different success criteria. A confidentiality result does not by itself authorize spending; a signature forgery does not by itself establish consensus capture; a hash result is not automatically a useful preimage.

For path `j`, define technical eligibility separately from economic outcomes:

* `K[j,k,v,g,t]`: the method applies to exact construction `k`, version `v`, and goal `g` at time `t`;
* `A[j,v,t]`: required attacker inputs and access are obtainable under the stated threat model;
* `R[j,t]`: the full resource vector fits the actor's capacity, budget and reliability constraints;
* `W[j,c,t]`: execution can complete while the affected interface/cohort is in the relevant vulnerable state;
* `V[j,v,t]`: where required by the goal, the output would satisfy the goal-specific technical validity/acceptability rule under the stated verifier and threat model if produced. It records a criterion, not an actual attempt or acceptance event.

`S_tech[j,c,t] = K ∩ A ∩ R ∩ W ∩ V` is a *technical eligibility event*. It does not assert an attack occurred, a candidate was submitted, a verifier accepted it, claims were lost, or a market reacted. Define `V` using the success criterion appropriate to the goal; for confidentiality or availability this need not be verifier acceptance.

Record an actual attempt and an accepted incident separately from eligibility. Let `O[j,v,c]` record whether an attempt occurred and its timestamp, and let `I[j,v,c]` record an independently evidenced actual goal-satisfying incident, with attempt time, incident time, version, goal, outcome, and verifier-acceptance status where applicable. `I=1` is an observation about an event; it is not included in `S_tech`. A failed or rejected attempt is recorded in `O` but not as a successful `I`.

Define the institutional mechanism `T[j,c,t]` from pre-existing or contemporaneously documented rules, not from realized loss. Examples include an enforceable custody obligation, redemption right, settlement rule, collateral contract, claim priority, or a documented balance-sheet link through which an accepted event could affect a claimant. Record mechanism type, parties, activation condition and timing. Do not define T as “loss happened.”

Define adaptation/containment state `C[j,c,t]` without reference to the outcome: e.g., whether an independently tested pause, patch, migration, rollback, key rotation, recovery channel or policy intervention is available and active, plus its measured coverage and activation time. It can be incomplete and can create costs or redistribute risk. Pre-register the efficacy criterion; the examples below use a generic fraction of pre-event exposure covered, not a universal threshold.

Define the pre-event exposed cohort `X0[c,j]` at a specified snapshot immediately before the trigger: the non-duplicated number or nominal value of claimant claims whose path depends on the exact target, net of already-effective fallback or exclusion. For valuation, use a fixed pre-trigger reference price and report units alongside dollars when relevant. Do not substitute market capitalization, total value locked, gross notional, affected signatures or intermediate counterparties for X0. If claims overlap, record each underlying claim once and map derivative/creditor claims separately.

Define the claimant outcome independently: `G[c,j; t0,t1]` is confirmed realized claim loss plus disjoint response/replacement expense, minus actual recovered value and non-overlapping value-preserving mitigation/substitution, measured over `[t0,t1]`. `Y[c,j; t0,t1] = max(0,G)` is net claimant economic burden. Report `Y` in claim units and `Y/X0` only when the denominator is observed and comparable; this burden-to-exposure ratio can exceed one because additional costs can exceed exposed claims. A market mark-to-market decline is a separate price outcome, not Y. Unadjudicated disputes, estimated exposures and missing reports remain separate categories. No numerical values for X0, G or Y are estimated in this paper.

### 3.2 Path diagram and probability discipline

```text
Capability evidence → exact-target technical eligibility S_tech
                              ↓ plus deployed exposure X0
             actual attempt O → goal-satisfying incident I (acceptance where relevant)
                              ↓ through documented T mechanism
     claimant economic burden Y, conditional on adaptation state C

Defender action updates C and X over time; attacker/market/policy actions also respond.
```

*Figure 1. Measurement architecture. Technical eligibility, deployment exposure, actual attempts and goal-satisfying incidents, institutional mechanism, adaptation state and claimant economic burden are distinct; arrows show a conditional measurement sequence, not independent probabilities or an estimated causal effect.*

The diagram is a measurement sequence, not a deterministic forecast. Technical feasibility, deployment exposure, an actual attempt, an actual goal-satisfying incident, T, C and Y are distinct variables. A technically eligible path may produce an observed incident only if it is attempted and the relevant goal-specific success conditions are met; verifier acceptance is recorded where relevant. An incident may affect claimants only through a documented mechanism and subject to adaptation/recovery. These are conditional questions for a path analysis, not empirical findings or the definition of Y.

If a later study models `S_tech`, the set-inclusion identity supplies an upper bound by each necessary gate, `P(S_tech) ≤ P(K), P(A), P(R), P(W), P(V)` for applicable gates. It supplies no probability without data. The chain rule uses conditional probabilities; multiplying marginal probabilities assumes a defended independence structure that is usually implausible. For multiple paths, `P(⋃S_tech,j) ≤ ΣP(S_tech,j)` but overlap can make equality false, and claimant losses cannot be added without de-duplication.

The four-state example used below demonstrates non-identification: equal weights on `{000,110,101,011}` and an independent uniform distribution on eight binary states both give each of three gates a 1/2 marginal and each pairwise intersection 1/4, but the triple intersection is respectively 0 and 1/8. This is an identity about joint distributions, not evidence about any attack. `T`, `C`, `X0` and `Y` must not be folded into the technical probability by assuming independence.

### 3.3 Adaptation is endogenous and multidimensional

Attacker and defender actions change the state. Disclosure may trigger inventory, code changes, wallet migration, key rotation, access restriction, emergency pauses, recovery or legal/policy action. These can reduce X, shorten W or change V and T; they can also create bugs, downtime, incompatibility, costs, or concentrated dependencies. Adaptation should therefore be observed as a dated sequence and coverage measure, not coded as a loss-free binary shield. A response undertaken after early losses can be endogenous to both severity and expectations. Conditioning on it does not identify its causal effect without an appropriate design.

## 4. Evidence classes and measurement contract

Evidence should be reported by the result achieved, exact target and source inspected. This paper distinguishes: (1) proposed theorem, conjecture or asymptotic method; (2) benchmark/reduced-instance result with task, parameters and verifier; (3) formal result applying to an exact family; (4) independently checked full-parameter result; (5) resource-feasible end-to-end attack against an exact deployed implementation; and (6) observed exploitation with documented claimant outcome. This is not a universal value ranking: a proof and an experiment answer different questions. An abstract, release note or citation is not interchangeable with a read primary body. Negative results require a described search or experiment; inaccessible material is not a negative result.

A reusable claim/evidence/measurement record should include:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Claim and type | Exact proposition; theorem, estimate, benchmark, author-reported run, official status, independent reproduction, incident or scenario. |
| Source identity | Authors/issuer, title, publication/version, date; journal vs preprint vs draft vs official status page. |
| Locator and retained body | URL or archive locator; printed/PDF page, section, figure, line or code object; body SHA-256 when bytes are retained. |
| What was read | Actual passages/pages/sections inspected; distinguish complete retention from complete reading. |
| Target scope | Primitive, parameters, implementation/version, interface, access model, attack goal and verifier. |
| Deployment/claim link | Cohort source, observation date, denominator, actual attempt (`O`) and goal-satisfying incident (`I`), with timestamps and acceptance status where relevant, contract or institutional mechanism, claims mapped. |
| Outcome and adaptation | Predefined X0, separate Y measure, recovery rules, C state/coverage, clocks and observation window. |
| Inference and countercase | Explicit inference, rival mechanism, uncertainty, missing source/data, and what would falsify or narrow the claim. |
| Reproduction | Actor/team, artifact, environment, resources, independent verifier, and result; “not checked” when applicable. |

The literature comparison draws on primary papers in security economics, reliability, standardization, banking liquidity, funding liquidity and information-theoretic secrecy. ### 4.2 Technical example: HAWK without scope inflation

Section 6.1 gives model-based gate-count estimates for HAWK-512 and HAWK-1024; the authors state that they did not attempt HAWK-512 key recovery. Section 6.2 reports a heuristic implementation that recovered two reference HAWK-256 keys and checked recovered keys with sign/verify. Appendix A describes first-party code and exact-lemma checks on small synthetic parameters. No cryptanalytic code or repository was executed for this revision.

## 5. Transition incentives: two architectures

Transition costs can delay adaptation even when an aggregate benefit is positive. To expose this mechanism, consider `n≥2` actors who choose `xᵢ∈{0,1}`, incur `cᵢ>0` if they act, and receive `bᵢ≥0` if protection succeeds. These are hypothetical common-horizon units, not dollars, probabilities, attack losses, or returns.

### 5.1 Every step is required

Architecture B has `g_B=∏ᵢxᵢ` and payoff `uᵢ=bᵢg_B−cᵢxᵢ`. It represents complementary indispensable steps, such as multiple parties that each must complete a migration for a stated service. All-zero is a strict pure equilibrium: one actor acting alone pays a cost without producing protection. All-one is a pure equilibrium iff every `bᵢ≥cᵢ`. No partial profile is a pure equilibrium because any active actor can withdraw, save cost, and leave protection absent. Aggregate welfare at all-one is `Σbᵢ−Σcᵢ`; it is socially preferred to no action iff this sum is positive. The social and individual conditions differ, so beneficial coordination can fail without a selection mechanism.

If transfers are enforceable and conditional on complete participation, they can support all-one exactly when total surplus `S=Σ(bᵢ−cᵢ)≥0`. One balanced transfer is `tᵢ=cᵢ−bᵢ+S/n`. This is an existence result under transferable utility, credible commitment, and no payment friction. It does not establish real-world willingness, ability to pay, or implementation. All-zero remains an equilibrium.

### 5.2 One provider protects all

Architecture E has `g_E=1` when any actor provides and payoff `uᵢ=bᵢg_E−cᵢxᵢ`. It is valid only if a provider really supplies the specified protection to all modeled beneficiaries. No provision is an equilibrium iff every `bᵢ≤cᵢ`. A singleton provider `j` is an equilibrium iff `bⱼ≥cⱼ`; additional providers would withdraw and free ride. Among nonempty profiles, the least-cost provider maximizes welfare, whose maximum is `max(0,Σbᵢ−minᵢcᵢ)`. Different providers can both be equilibria even though one is socially more efficient.

With `b=(2,2)` and `c=(3,3)`, no provider is the only pure equilibrium although one provider creates welfare 1. This shows possible underprovision under the model, not inevitable protocol failure. With `b=(6,4)` and `c=(5,1)`, either singleton is an equilibrium, but the costly provider yields welfare 5 while the low-cost provider yields welfare 9. It would be wrong to claim that provision is worse than no provision: the comparison is between providers. Under nonnegative benefits, a private provider that satisfies `bⱼ≥cⱼ` cannot have negative modeled welfare relative to no provision.

These architectures inherit familiar complementary-effort and public-goods/reliability logic (including the total-effort, weakest-link and best-shot taxonomy discussed by Varian). They are intentionally different stylized cases, not novel general theorems. Some migrations are complementary; some services can be shared; many real systems combine quorum, fallback, heterogeneous custody and optional participation. A wallet-key migration is not automatically a one-provider public good. Architecture must be measured before choosing a game.

### 5.3 Checks and boundaries

The archived CTE model reported 2,328 parameter instances and 37,248 profile checks, including a seeded four-player sample; those remain historical outputs and were not rerun. The portable `reproducibility/replay.py` supplied with this revision programmatically checks the P3 joint-distribution witness, B/E pure equilibria and welfare on 1,872 cases per architecture, the transfer construction in 649 nonnegative-surplus cases, and four interior P6 mixed-indifference examples. It visits 28,800 candidate action profiles across B and E; this is not a count of deviation checks. Its domain is only n=2,3, benefits 0–3, costs 1–3. These finite consistency checks do not estimate adoption, attack feasibility or market response.

## 6. From technical eligibility to claimant outcomes

A complete path analysis is a set of linked evidence questions, with measurement kept separate from inference.

**Target and technical eligibility.** Name exact construction, parameter set, implementation/version, interface, access assumptions, attack goal and verifier. Separate a theorem about a family, a modeled resource cost, a benchmark result, a reproduced instance and an operational attack. State the relevant clock and whether execution could fit within the time X remains exposed. A smaller challenge instance does not transfer automatically to production parameters.

**Pre-event exposure.** Identify claimants and the exact dependency they rely on. Freeze `X0` at a timestamp before the trigger; document source, units, valuation rule, fallback treatment and deduplication. Separate addresses or interfaces that differ in key exposure, custody, script type, reuse or migration path. Missing or inferred exposure is a range or unknown, not zero and not total market capitalization.

**Goal validity, incident and institutional transmission.** Define the goal-specific validity/acceptability criterion (`V`) under the stated rules without presuming that an attempt occurred. Record an actual attempt (`O`) and its outcome; record any goal-satisfying incident (`I`) with its own timestamp and evidence, including verifier acceptance where relevant. Then identify the rule, contract or balance-sheet link (`T`) that could affect a claim. This may be protocol finality, custody duty, redemption terms, collateral contract or a court/administrative remedy. Record parties, conditions, settlement time and dispute rights. T describes the mechanism, not the loss realized under it.

**Adaptation and recovery.** Record discovery, public disclosure, responder decision, patch/activation, migration coverage, pause, reversal, recovery and compensation as separate dates. `C` is measured from active controls and coverage, independently of Y. A control can block, delay or redirect a path; it may shift costs to another group. A status announcement alone does not prove deployment. Record actual recoveries and whether they overlap with substitute value. Recovery and post-disclosure intervention are part of the outcome/adaptation history, not baseline confounders to condition away in the primary H1 specification.

**Outcome and aggregation.** Calculate claimant-level net economic burden `Y` from confirmed realized claim loss plus disjoint response/replacement expense, less actual recovered value and non-overlapping value-preserving mitigation. Because costs may exceed `X0`, report `Y/X0` as a burden-to-exposure ratio that may exceed one, not as a bounded share. Reconcile linked users, custodians, lenders, exchanges, derivative counterparties and issuer obligations so that one economic loss is not counted at every layer. Report nominal claim loss, realized loss, mark-to-market changes and social welfare separately. A system-level label such as “collapse” must be operationalized before analysis; it cannot stand in for these measures.

**Timing and causal interpretation.** A capability release may follow confidential disclosure or a previous exploit. Market repricing can precede public reporting. A pause may occur after partial losses. Record public, private and incident clocks if known; do not treat a single post date as attack onset. A cross-sectional association among exposure, controls and Y can reverse causal order. Evidence for one link does not automatically support the next.

## 7. Countercases and nonmonotonic outcomes

Several countercases bound the thesis.

First, Shannon’s perfect-secrecy result shows that a uniformly random independent one-use `n`-bit key makes `C=M⊕K` statistically independent of `M`: for every `m,c`, `P(C=c|M=m)=2⁻ⁿ`, so observing ciphertext does not change the message posterior. Additional computation alone does not alter this equality. This is a narrow confidentiality example, not a deployed cryptocurrency design, authentication scheme, or claim that implementation leakage is impossible. It refutes the universal slogan “enough intelligence decodes every ciphertext,” not the possibility of particular cryptographic failures.

Second, migration can precede attack capability. Candidate withdrawal, standards selection, software inventory and staged deployment can remove a vulnerable path before it becomes exploitable. Conversely, emergency migration can introduce bugs or interoperability failures. Both effects need observation.

Third, defender-side AI may accelerate code discovery, test coverage, cryptographic agility, and incident response. It may also generate insecure migrations or make offensive research cheaper. Without matched data, the net effect is unknown. A model could improve both sides while lowering loss if governance and response improve faster; it could worsen loss if the relevant offensive gate crosses first. Neither direction follows from capability headlines.

Fourth, exposure may be localized. Compromised signatures may affect only certain address types, key states, protocols, or time windows. Assets can be paused, recovered, contested, or migrated; economic claims may overlap. A large headline value is not a realized loss.

Finally, policy can redirect who bears losses. A freeze, fork, compensation scheme, liability rule, exchange delisting, or public guarantee can reduce one claimant’s loss and impose costs elsewhere. This resembles the asymmetry lesson from contingent investments: the apparent upside is not the same as an attractive net payoff after price, carry, funding, and collection. In the present paper there is no authorized trade expression and no investor-specific recommendation. No qualifying short expression is established; holding, cash or other comparison cases remain hypothetical and depend on the relevant mandate and terms.

## 8. Synthetic measurement example: gates are not outcomes

Suppose a fictional cohort has `X0=40` exposed claim units just before a public, verified disclosure. An independently reproducible attack would—if it succeeds and is accepted—affect 25% of those units in one specified branch. That branch's gross claimant loss is `40 × 0.25 = 10` units. It also incurs one disjoint unit of replacement expense; six units are actually recovered; no overlapping substitution value is credited. Then `G=10+1−6=5` and `Y=5` units, or `Y/X0=0.125` (12.5%). All inputs are hypothetical. The result is not an estimate of probability, real exposure, attack severity, recovery or loss. It is only the arithmetic for one scenario branch.

For that branch, `S_tech` eligibility could be true while Y remains zero if no claim is realized or a control interrupts transmission. Conversely, the existence of T does not fix the amount of Y: contract interpretation, claimant behavior, delays, intervention, overlapping rights and actual recoveries still matter. The state variables must be observed and linked rather than collapsed into one “risk score.”

If someone assigned marginal probabilities `.4,.5,.5,.5,.5` to K, A, R, W and V and multiplied them, `.025` would encode an independence assumption. It would not be a measured attack probability. The binary example in §3 shows that even equal marginals and pairwise independence fail to identify a joint event. Without defensible joint data, report a conditional scenario and leave its probability null. A committee might still fund inventory, migration testing or recovery planning because those steps have option value across scenarios; such precaution does not mean the event is likely.

For an investor, even a well-supported conditional loss scenario is not an attractive investment by itself. One must compare the market's implicit distribution with research assumptions, quantify price and carry, identify permitted instruments, and stress financing, liquidity, collateral, counterparty and collection. A correct macro intuition can have negative expected value after costs or be inaccessible. This paper offers no current instrument, quote, funding term or portfolio expression; its expression conclusion is null.

## 9. What should count as economic transmission?

The word “collapse” can refer to several observably different events: code-level failure, interruption of transaction processing, loss of token market value, inability to redeem a claim, insolvency of an intermediary, or a cascade across contracts. A study should choose one outcome before looking at data. These outcomes may co-occur, but one does not define another.

For an authorization attack, the first economic object might be an invalid transfer accepted by a particular verifier. The next is a claimant ledger: which user loses control, whether the transaction is final, whether a freeze or fork can reverse it, and whether a custodian owes reimbursement. Only after that can one examine market-making, collateral calls, redemptions, lending losses, or runs. Gross token capitalization is a market valuation of all outstanding units, not the cash amount stolen or the liability available to absorb losses. Aggregating it as “exposure” risks counting the same underlying coin across owner, exchange, lender, and derivative claims.

Run theory is relevant only under a matching balance-sheet structure. A queue of users wanting to exit an asset does not by itself prove maturity transformation. The researcher must establish what is redeemable on demand, who holds liquid reserves, which assets are slow to sell, the order of claims, and whether actions by early redeemers reduce value for later ones. Funding-liquidity stress requires identified collateral, margin or rollover demands; network contagion needs a path from one institution’s impairment to another’s. The CTE archive itself cautions that the selected theoretical papers support conditional mechanisms, not crypto-specific crash probabilities.

For each alleged channel, a table should record the initiator, contract or protocol rule, affected balance-sheet line, time to realization, competing response, recovery source, and double-counting risk. If one row cannot be completed from evidence, the channel remains a hypothesis. This format also captures losses that do not appear in token prices, such as delayed service, legal expense, forced migration, or unreimbursed custodian liability, while preventing every transition cost from being described as catastrophic destruction.

## 10. Identification strategy and preregistration contract

A descriptive reporting protocol can be used before a causal study exists. Each event should have a source-complete claim ledger, separate capability/deployment/claims tables, explicit event clocks and a deduplicated claimant register. Missing source documents and disputed claims should remain visibly missing or disputed. A journal-style case narrative is not a dataset. If several reports share an author, model, code or source, they are correlated evidence, not independent replications.

A possible study of adaptation lag should pre-register: exact construction, version, goal and cohort; the date of first public verified disclosure; inclusion and exclusion rules; `X0` source and units; the independent method for verifying feasibility; the definition of effective adaptation and cohort coverage; the outcome adjudication source; the recovery and substitution ledger; and a fixed outcome window. The eligibility flow must distinguish technical eligibility, actual attempts `O`, and goal-satisfying incidents `I`; benchmark or modeled capability alone cannot establish an incident. Record separate discovery, attempt, incident, disclosure and adaptation clocks. The primary disclosure cohort should exclude episodes with a material incident or adaptation before `t0`, or place them in a separately preregistered clock stratum; publish the screened-in, excluded, disputed and missing counts so selection into verified/public incidents is visible. For cross-episode comparability, the proposed primary endpoint below uses 365 days after disclosure; shorter windows (30, 90 and 180 days) are sensitivity descriptions, not replacements selected after observing the result. If any outcome is right-censored, report it rather than coding it zero.

The proposed outcome is `Y365/X0`: confirmed net claimant economic burden over `[t0,t0+365d]` divided by exposure measured immediately before `t0`. It includes disjoint response/replacement expense net of actual recovery and non-overlapping value-preserving mitigation, and may exceed one. Report monetary units and claim counts separately. Distinguish exploit transactions, direct claim settlement, response expense, recoveries, mitigation/substitution, and price declines. Mark-to-market return can be a separate secondary outcome with its own benchmark and window. The adaptation lag is days from `t0` to the first independently evidenced date when a pre-specified share—suggested as 90% for the primary design—of X0 is covered by effective migration, pause or containment. The 90% threshold is a protocol choice, not a natural constant; disclose coverage continuously and repeat at other thresholds. If the threshold is never met within 365 days, lag is right-censored at that endpoint.

A candidate comparator is a different independently documented incident cohort using the same goal and material dependency class, exposed under a comparable verifier/settlement architecture, but with shorter measured adaptation lag. Exclude pure theory or benchmark claims without a deployed, independently validated resource-feasible path; cohorts without an auditable pre-event denominator; confidentiality-only events when the primary endpoint is unauthorized claim loss; duplicate or overlapping episodes counted as independent; and events with no dated public verified disclosure or no outcome attribution window. Publish a flow table for screened-in, excluded, disputed and missing episodes. Exclusion for non-comparability must not silently remove high-loss cases.

Causal inference is not automatic. Severity, prior incidents, development capacity, governance quality, protocol size, custody structure, market conditions and policy intervention may affect both lag and Y. Response can be triggered by early losses; selection into public disclosure can depend on severity. Conditioning on a verified incident can itself induce selection, and requiring a full 365-day observable outcome can select against recent or poorly reported episodes. Show the full eligibility and follow-up flow and compare included with excluded episodes on available pre-disclosure characteristics. A lag not reached by day 365 is right-censored, not an exact 365-day duration; retain that status and use an estimator appropriate to censoring or report only a clearly labeled descriptive case series. Because Y accrues during the same 365-day interval in which adaptation lag is measured, longer lag mechanically leaves more time with residual exposure. The primary coefficient, if data ever support one, is a cumulative-window association; it is not an effect independent of time at risk, and should not be presented as a standalone predictor or causal effect. Separate outcomes before and after effective containment where ascertainable, report coverage as a time-varying history, and state how competing events (pause, migration, recovery, rollback) are treated. Early loss can also accelerate adaptation, creating reverse causation. With few events, insufficient covariate overlap, selection concerns or thin outcome data, report a case series and do not estimate H1. A panel/event study would need dated announcements, affected and unaffected control cohorts, market controls, prespecified event windows and placebo tests; it estimates announcement-associated repricing, not exploitation probability or claimant loss. Difference-in-differences needs defensible parallel trends and exogenous timing; an instrument needs a credible exclusion restriction. Neither is supplied here.

Useful negative controls include an unaffected cryptographic goal, implementation versions not using the implicated primitive, pre-disclosure outcome windows, placebo disclosure dates and assets without the relevant interface. Sensitivity analyses should vary exposure classification, disputed claims, denominator valuation, the 90% threshold and the recovery window. Report failed source retrievals, unverifiable implementation state and incomplete claims records. An AI-performance study should additionally hold task, access, tools, oracle feedback, compute, wall clock and verifier constant; include all attempts and failures, distinguish candidate from checked solutions, and test whether assistance changes migration or recovery as well as attack-task outcomes.

## 11. Decision use under deep uncertainty

The framework can guide low-regret preparation even when probabilities are unavailable. Inventorying cryptographic dependencies, identifying upgrade authority, testing fallback paths, and rehearsing recovery may have option value because they improve response across multiple threat scenarios. Their costs and side effects still matter. A migration may create interoperability failures, new trusted components, concentrated vendor dependence, or bugs. A pause may preserve value but impose availability losses. The decision compares these consequences against a range of conditional scenarios rather than pretending that a point probability is known.

For decision analysis, a scenario table separates research assumptions from market assumptions. Research assumptions include target applicability, access, execution resources, exposed cohort, severity, recovery, adaptation speed, and policy response. Market assumptions include instrument terms, bid/ask, implied distribution, borrow, funding, collateral, counterparty credit, and exit liquidity. The two sets can interact, but a market price is not a direct measurement of a path probability. Nor does a low premium establish that funding or collection risk is small.

The current paper has no public-market price or authorized expression in scope. It therefore cannot conclude that a short position is attractive. No qualifying short expression is established; holding, cash or other comparison cases remain hypothetical and depend on the relevant mandate and terms. Any future expression would need to show that net expected payoff survives price movement, carry, collateral calls, liquidity gaps, wrong-way counterparty risk, policy intervention, and a sequence of small losses. A hypothetical maximum payout cannot substitute for that portfolio survival analysis.

## 12. Falsifiable hypotheses and research design

The complete path description in §3 is a necessary-condition checklist, not H1. The empirical H1 is associational unless a credible identification strategy supports causal language:

> **H1 (adaptation-lag association):** Among incident episodes with an independently validated, resource-feasible attack applicable to a specified deployed dependency and attack goal, a longer interval from first public verified disclosure (`t0`) to effective migration or containment is associated with a higher 365-day ratio of confirmed net claimant economic burden (`Y365`) to the independently measured pre-disclosure exposed-cohort denominator (`X0`), comparing otherwise similar episodes and after pre-specified adjustment for pre-disclosure target severity, exposed-interface fraction, access, settlement rules and baseline controls. Post-disclosure recovery and intervention are recorded as outcome/adaptation histories, not adjusted away in the primary specification.

This comparison uses `t0` for all included episodes; adaptation lag is days from `t0` until at least 90% of `X0` is effectively covered, censored at day 365. The denominator is valued immediately before `t0`, with units also reported. The endpoint includes disjoint response/replacement costs net of actual recoveries and non-overlapping value-preserving mitigation; it is a burden-to-exposure ratio, not a bounded share, and may exceed one. Price drawdown is not part of the endpoint. The inclusion/exclusion and comparator rules are specified in §10. The 365-day window and 90% coverage threshold are proposed protocol choices to preregister before data collection, not results or findings. If material attack or adaptation preceded public disclosure, record the separate private/incident dates; exclude from the primary public-disclosure analysis or report in a prespecified alternate-clock stratum.

H1 states a proposed positive cumulative-window association, not a causal effect or an independently validated prediction. Longer lag can be a proxy for greater severity, weaker governance, more complex interfaces or low development capacity; early losses can accelerate adaptation. It also mechanically lengthens the period in which residual exposure can generate the 365-day outcome. The primary adjustment set uses only pre-disclosure covariates. Post-disclosure recovery, intervention and adaptation may mediate or form part of the endpoint and are reported descriptively/decomposed rather than conditioned away. Unobserved confounding, measurement error, survivorship, incident selection, disclosure selection and right-censoring can bias either direction. With too few independent episodes, no overlap in covariates, censored lag not handled, or no auditable Y/X0, H1 is not estimated. A null or negative association, shorter-lag cohorts with systematically higher outcomes, or loss ratios unrelated to lag under reliable measurement would weaken the proposed association. A causal interpretation or claim of predictive value would require a separate defensible identification and validation design; none is claimed here.

Related descriptive questions remain open: which migration tasks are complementary versus shared; whether identified claims have a measurable institutional transmission route; whether defensive AI reduces defect discovery or migration time; and whether policy reduces one claimant's loss while shifting cost to another. These are questions, not additional confirmed hypotheses. They require independently collected actor, cost, deployment, exposure, recovery and control data.

The causal path itself has clear falsifiers for any studied instance: the primary body does not support the asserted method; independent application to exact parameters fails; the target/version is not deployed in the cohort; resources or access do not fit the claimed window; the verifier rejects the output; the exposed denominator is immaterial or not attributable; active adaptation closes the interface; or the defined claimant burden is fully offset across all components—claim loss, response/replacement costs, recoveries and non-overlapping mitigation—so that `Y=0`. A cryptographic finding can remain important while the proposed economic path is falsified.

## 13. Limitations, provenance and declarations

This is a selective synthesis and proposed reporting protocol, not a comprehensive systematic review or empirical study. No attack probability, arrival date, exposed share, adaptation-lag coefficient, AI offense/defense effect, market impact or net loss is estimated. No current network inventory, account data, portfolio, private balances, trading instrument, live price, counterparty terms or collection route is included. The games assume discrete actions, known fixed benefits/costs and specified protection architectures; actual governance includes heterogeneous users, information, contracts, competing safeguards, strategic timing and uncertainty. The finite replay covers only stated integer parameter grids; it does not test all proofs, equilibrium refinements, mixed equilibria, deployed adoption or a cryptographic attack.

Source versions, exact locators, retained-body hashes where available, reading limits and retrieval exclusions are documented in the public source ledger (`evidence/working-paper-sources.json`). The manuscript relies on bounded primary-source readings; it is not a systematic review. HAWK theorem, modeled costs, author-run recoveries, participant statements and administrative status remain distinct evidence classes. No HAWK attack code or repository was inspected or executed. A post-revision internal review checked the definitions, mathematical claims and selected retained primary passages; its scope and qualifications are recorded in the companion review package. These AI-assisted reviews share model and source context and are not external scholarly peer review. External review by cryptography, economics and financial-stability specialists is absent. No journal-guide compliance, human novice test, external peer review or submission is claimed.

## 14. Conclusion

An attack result does not alone establish a claimant loss, market collapse or investable payoff. Each path must connect exact technical eligibility to an observed exposed cohort, an independently measurable institutional mechanism, an adaptation state and a claimant outcome that is not built into the gate definition. A reporting protocol can make those gaps visible, while established work supplies much of the economics and game structure. The modest contribution here is to align source maturity, clocks, denominators, controls, claims and recoveries in one reusable ledger.

There is no estimated H1 association, no calibrated probability and no trade. The most informative next study is bounded: choose one documented deployed dependency and goal, independently validate the technical path, define the cohort and pre-event X0, record public and private clocks, test the actual settlement mechanism, and trace 365-day claimant outcomes and adaptation coverage. If comparable episodes are too few, publish a transparent case series and leave H1 unestimated. Do not infer probability or collapse from capability headlines.
