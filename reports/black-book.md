# The Black Book
## Company research and conditional crypto-risk mechanisms

**The Big Crypto Short - Public research edition - 14 November 2026**

This casebook brings together 22 core company cases and ten separately qualified auxiliary studies from the internally reviewed research edition. It traces dated observations, contractual mechanisms, competing explanations and missing evidence. A historical blacklist or watchlist label describes research classification; it does not establish a current security, an attractive price, a live option contract or a trade recommendation. Zero names in this edition are verified trade-ready.

Personal portfolio chapters, holdings, account balances, account-registration details and tailored broker-process material are excluded from this public edition. The private lab preserves those originals and every draft. This edition changes selection and presentation, not the underlying company calculations or conclusions. Evidence dates and review limitations remain part of each case.

The research is AI-assisted and internally challenged. Shared sources and model families limit the independence of that process; this is not an external audit or scholarly peer review. No current network compromise, calibrated crash probability or executable trade is established.

## ANTA — Antalpha Platform Holding Company

The FY2025 Form 20-F describes financing tied to Bitcoin-mining-machine purchases and warns that a material decline in that market could harm results. It also reports three borrowers at 74% of total loan receivables and two customers each above 10% of annual revenue at December 31, 2025. A loan table shows approximately US$1.001bn net receivables, including US$443.904m from customers and US$556.920m due from related parties; the latter is 55.6% of total net loans. That concentration and related-party balance are relevant to collection, but they are not evidence of default. The 20-F also reports US$1.257bn of crypto-collateral receivables from a related party (52.2% of total assets), alongside separate customer/related-party collateral payables and related-party loan payables. The note explains that customer Bitcoin is rehypothecated to funding partner Northstar, transferred to a Northstar-designated address, and returned in like quantity/type only after full repayment. Northstar must provide monitoring access and may not transfer without written consent; the issuer disclosed no borrowing defaults at Dec. 31, 2025. Proprietary XAUt collateral is a separate gross position without a matching offset. Those contractual controls are an important countercase, while lender custody and the repayment-before-return sequence are a collectibility dependency. The annual balance sheet is dated and no current borrower aging, loan terms, liens or recovery data were established.

A crypto-only downturn could reduce miner cash generation, machine residual values, and new financing demand. It does not establish losses on loans if machine collateral is enforceable, advance rates conservative, and liquidation recoveries timely. In a technical or enforcement event, collateral title, perfection, priority, cross-border seizure, and custodian access may matter more than the collateral label. In broad contagion, borrower and collateral values may decline together, increasing wrong-way risk. The strongest countercase is secured lending with stable repayments, low realized losses, and recoverable machines; fee income and borrower demand could persist through a moderate cycle. None of those defenses can be confirmed from the annual concentration tables alone. No current deterioration conclusion follows from FY2025 values. The next bounded question is whether a latest-period aging and collateral schedule identifies the related-party balance, borrower defaults, lien priority, expected liquidation cost, and realized recovery history. No security-specific downside or trade is supported.

Its March 31 statements report that wholly owned subsidiary Prestige Wealth Management Limited borrowed $50 million from related-party Northstar Digital (HK) Limited for three years at 6%, with principal and accrued interest payable as a lump sum at maturity. The filing reports first-priority perfected liens on XAUt then worth $66.667 million. It says the lender must provide monitoring access, cannot move collateral to another address without prior written consent, and must return the same quantity and type only upon full repayment. These are issuer-reported terms; the agreements, lien opinion, collateral statement, closeout sequence and parent guarantee were not reviewed. A right to receive the same number of tokens after full repayment does not show that repayment and release occur simultaneously or that the lender can perform in stress.

At June 30, 2026, Aurelion’s unaudited consolidated statements report $34.790 million direct XAUt, $32.020 million XAUE, $66.388 million related-party XAUt collateral receivable, $1.592 million cash, $42.793 million related-party loan payable, $44.437 million total liabilities and $90.901 million GAAP equity. The three gold-linked rows sum to $133.198 million; this is reported book value, not collected cash. Aurelion reported 33,318 XAUt units including 8,000 staked in XAUE. At the reported approximate $3,996 per unit, 33,318 units imply $133.139 million, only $59,567 below the row sum. That close arithmetic suggests alignment with reported exposure, not proof of legal non-overlap or control. The receivable is 1.55 times the loan and cash is 3.72% of that debt; neither is a recovery ratio. March 31 cash flow showed $2.909 million operating cash use and $21.337 million unrealized XAUt/collateral gain. The gain is not cash, and the March flow period cannot be combined with June cash to assert a runway.

The strongest survival case is: gold may retain value; Tether may honor XAUt claims; Aurelion may have reliable operational access; pledged tokens may be monitored and returned; XAUE may redeem as described; and debt may be serviced on schedule. June reported equity was positive. AURE’s exposure can therefore survive token-market stress, and none of the inspected evidence establishes a default, custody shortfall, missed redemption, fraud or stock-price beta. A share is an equity residual in a Cayman parent, not a token redemption ticket. No evidence reviewed establishes parent guarantee, direct shareholder redemption rights or which subsidiary controls each token/cash receipt.

Independent arithmetic checks reconstruct June assets of $135.339 million and equity of $90.901 million. Imposed 25% impairment of the $66.388 million collateral receivable alone would reduce book equity by $16.597 million (18.3%) if fully recognized and all else unchanged; it is a sensitivity, not a probability or predicted loss. A separate 25% equal-gold mark shock across all three lines would be $33.300 million only if the rows are distinct and all equally price-sensitive. Neither calculation estimates AURE’s stock return. A decline in gold, token discount, access blockage and failed collection are different mechanisms.

I would falsify the collection concern with current executed Tether and XAUE terms, holder-level redemption evidence, independent bar-level custody and title records, current lien/perfection and collateral statements, proof of enforceable return/settlement and adequate borrower liquidity through maturity. Missing are those documents, later unit/cash/debt roll-forward, collateral address/encumbrance records, contractual creditor priority and parent/subsidiary cash allocation. Confidence is moderate in the reproduced issuer-reported historical amounts and low in collectibility because operative rights and settlement are unresolved. The next bounded question is: **what exact entities and clauses govern redemption and return of AURE’s current XAUt, and has any comparable position actually been redeemed and received as cash or bullion?** Until that is answered, no qualifying trade and no adoption.
